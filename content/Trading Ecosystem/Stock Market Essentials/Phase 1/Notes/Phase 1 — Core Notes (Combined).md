# 1.1 — Income, Expenses & Savings Rate

**Phase:** [[📘 Phase 1 MOC — Financial Foundation]]

---

## What is a Savings Rate?

Savings Rate = (Money Saved ÷ Total Income) × 100

**Example:**
- Monthly Income: ₹50,000
- Monthly Expenses: ₹35,000
- Savings: ₹15,000
- Savings Rate = 30%

A savings rate of **20% or more** is the minimum to build wealth.

---

## Why It Matters for Investing

You can only invest money you don't spend.
Before picking stocks, you need to know:
- How much comes in
- How much goes out
- How much is left to invest

---

## Simple Budget Framework

| Category | % of Income |
|----------|------------|
| Needs (rent, food, bills) | 50% |
| Wants (eating out, shopping) | 30% |
| Savings + Investments | 20% |

This is the **50-30-20 Rule**. It's a starting point, not a law.

---

## Action Step

Write down your:
1. Monthly take-home salary
2. Fixed expenses (rent, EMI, bills)
3. Variable expenses (food, transport, entertainment)
4. What's left over

**That leftover amount = your investable surplus.**

# 1.2 — Emergency Fund — What, Why & How Much

**Phase:** [[📘 Phase 1 MOC — Financial Foundation]]

---

## What is an Emergency Fund?

Money set aside for unexpected events:
- Job loss
- Medical emergency
- Urgent travel
- Major repairs

It is **NOT** an investment. It is a safety net.

---

## How Much Should You Keep?

**Rule:** 3 to 6 months of your monthly expenses.

If your monthly expenses = ₹30,000
→ Emergency fund target = ₹90,000 to ₹1,80,000

---

## Where to Keep It?

| Option | Why It Works |
|--------|-------------|
| High-interest savings account | Instantly accessible |
| Liquid Mutual Fund | Slightly better returns, withdraw in 1 day |
| FD with sweep-in facility | Good returns, can break instantly |

**NOT in:** Stocks, Crypto, or any market-linked instrument.

---

## Why This Comes BEFORE Investing

Without an emergency fund, you will be **forced to sell your investments** at the worst time (when the market is down, and you are also in crisis).

That is how wealth is destroyed — not by bad stocks, but by bad financial planning.

# 1.6 — What is Compounding (With Indian Examples)

**Phase:** [[📘 Phase 1 MOC — Financial Foundation]]

---

## The One Concept That Changes Everything

Compounding = **Your returns earning more returns.**

Simple interest: You earn on the original amount only.
Compound interest: You earn on the original amount **+ all previous returns.**

---

## Example in Numbers

You invest ₹1,00,000 at 12% annual return (NIFTY 50 long-term average):

| Year | Value |
|------|-------|
| 1 | ₹1,12,000 |
| 5 | ₹1,76,234 |
| 10 | ₹3,10,585 |
| 20 | ₹9,64,629 |
| 30 | ₹29,95,992 |

You put in ₹1 lakh. 30 years later without adding a single rupee: **₹29.9 lakhs.**

---

## The Real Magic: SIP + Compounding

If you invest ₹5,000/month in a NIFTY 50 index fund at 12% average annual return:

| Years | Money Invested | Wealth Created |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| 10 | ₹6,00,000 | ₹11.6 Lakhs |
| 20 | ₹12,00,000 | ₹49.9 Lakhs |
| 30 | ₹18,00,000 | ₹1.76 Crore |

**The longer you stay, the less you need to invest.**

---

## Why Starting Early is the #1 Rule

Starting at 22 vs 32 — the difference is **massive.**
Time in market > Timing the market.

# 1.7 — Inflation — The Hidden Tax on Your Savings

**Phase:** [[📘 Phase 1 MOC — Financial Foundation]]

---

## What is Inflation?

Inflation = prices rising every year.

What ₹100 buys today ≠ what ₹100 buys in 10 years.

India's average inflation = 5–6% per year.

---

## Why This Destroys FD Returns

FD interest rate = 6.5%
Inflation = 5.5%
**Real return = 1%**

After tax (30% bracket), FD return = ~4.5%
After inflation = **negative real return.**

Your money is losing value in the FD.

---

## The Solution: Equities (Stocks)

NIFTY 50 historical average return = 12–14% per year.
After 6% inflation = 6–8% real return.

That is **real wealth creation.**

# 1.15 — What is a Mutual Fund (Explained Simply)

**Phase:** [[📘 Phase 1 MOC — Financial Foundation]]

---

## The Simple Analogy

Imagine 1,000 people each put ₹1,000 into a common pool = ₹10,00,000.
A professional fund manager takes that ₹10 lakh and invests it across 50 stocks.
All 1,000 people share the profits and losses proportionally.

**That pool = Mutual Fund.**
**Your ₹1,000 share = 1 Unit.**

---

## Types You Need to Know (For Now)

| Type | What it Does | Risk |
|------|-------------|------|
| Index Fund | Copies NIFTY 50 | Low-Medium |
| Large Cap Fund | Invests in big companies | Medium |
| Mid Cap Fund | Invests in mid-size companies | Medium-High |
| Small Cap Fund | Invests in small companies | High |
| Liquid Fund | Invests in short-term bonds | Low |

---

## Key Terms

- **NAV (Net Asset Value):** Price of 1 unit of the fund
- **AUM:** Total money managed by the fund
- **Expense Ratio:** Annual fee charged by fund (keep below 0.5% for index funds)
- **Exit Load:** Fee if you exit too early

---

## For Beginners: Start With Index Funds Only

Why?
- No fund manager risk
- Lowest expense ratio (0.1%)
- Proven long-term performance
- Simple to understand
