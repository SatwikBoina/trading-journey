## 🧠 Module 3 Key Concepts

| Concept | One-Line Explanation |
|---------|---------------------|
| CPI | Measures price rise at the consumer (retail) level — RBI's primary target |
| WPI | Measures price rise at the wholesale/factory level |
| Core Inflation | CPI excluding food and fuel — shows underlying inflation trend |
| Headline Inflation | Total CPI including food and fuel — the number media reports |
| Demand-Pull | Inflation caused by strong demand (economy overheating) |
| Cost-Push | Inflation caused by rising input costs (oil, raw materials) |
| Stagflation | High inflation + low growth — worst of both worlds |
| Real Return | What you actually earn after subtracting inflation |
| Pricing Power | Company's ability to raise prices without losing customers |

## 🔗 The Inflation → Market Chain

```
CPI inflation rises above RBI's 4% target
  ↓
RBI raises repo rate (to reduce demand)
  ↓
Borrowing costs rise → spending falls → economy cools
  ↓
Company earnings growth slows
  ↓
NIFTY falls, especially rate-sensitive sectors

BUT:
  ↓
Companies with strong pricing power (Asian Paints, Nestle, Titan)
→ Can pass price increases to consumers
→ Margins protected → stocks relatively resilient
```

---

## 📊 India's Inflation Target

RBI has a **flexible inflation targeting framework:**
- Target: **4% CPI** (with tolerance band of 2–6%)
- If CPI stays above 6% for 3 consecutive quarters → RBI MUST raise rates

Track: mospi.gov.in → CPI release (monthly, released around 12th of next month)
