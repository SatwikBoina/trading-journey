# 4.1 — What is a Candlestick and How to Read It

**Phase:** [[📒 Phase 4 MOC — Technical Analysis & Timing]]

---

## What is a Candlestick?

A candlestick shows **4 pieces of price information** for a given time period (1 day, 1 hour, 15 min, etc.):

```
    |       ← High (highest price in the period)
   ███      ← Body (from Open to Close)
   ███
    |       ← Low (lowest price in the period)
```

- **Green/White candle:** Price went UP (Close > Open)
- **Red/Black candle:** Price went DOWN (Close < Open)

---

## Reading a Daily Candlestick

Example: Reliance on 15 Jan 2024
- Open: ₹2,800
- High: ₹2,870
- Low: ₹2,780
- Close: ₹2,850

The body goes from ₹2,800 (open) to ₹2,850 (close) → green candle.
The wick at top goes up to ₹2,870 (sellers pushed it back down slightly).
The wick at bottom goes to ₹2,780 (buyers stepped in).

---

## What Candles Tell You

| Feature | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| Long green body | Strong buying pressure |
| Long red body | Strong selling pressure |
| Long upper wick | Sellers rejecting high prices |
| Long lower wick | Buyers stepping in at low prices |
| Small body | Indecision — balance of buyers & sellers |

# 4.8 — Support and Resistance — The Foundation

**Phase:** [[📒 Phase 4 MOC — Technical Analysis & Timing]]

---

## What is Support?

**Support = A price level where buyers typically show up.**

The price falls to this level, buyers step in, price bounces back up.
Think of it as a floor that holds the price.

---

## What is Resistance?

**Resistance = A price level where sellers typically show up.**

The price rises to this level, sellers step in, price falls back.
Think of it as a ceiling that blocks the price.

---

## How to Draw Support & Resistance

1. Look for areas where price reversed multiple times
2. The more times a level has been tested, the stronger it is
3. Round numbers often act as S/R (₹500, ₹1000, ₹1500)

---

## The Role Reversal Rule

When price **breaks through resistance**, that resistance **becomes support.**
When price **breaks through support**, that support **becomes resistance.**

This is one of the most important rules in technical analysis.

---

## Practical Application

- Buy near support, sell near resistance = basic trading logic
- If price breaks out above resistance with high volume → strong signal to buy

# 4.21 — Moving Averages — 20, 50, 200 EMA

**Phase:** [[📒 Phase 4 MOC — Technical Analysis & Timing]]

---

## What is a Moving Average?

A line that shows the **average price over a given number of days**, updated every day.

**20 EMA** = average of last 20 days' prices (short-term)
**50 EMA** = average of last 50 days' prices (medium-term)
**200 EMA** = average of last 200 days' prices (long-term trend)

EMA = Exponential Moving Average. It gives more weight to recent prices. Preferred over simple MA.

---

## How to Use Each

| Moving Average | Primary Use |
|---------------|------------|
| 20 EMA | Short-term trend direction for swing trades |
| 50 EMA | Medium-term support/resistance level |
| 200 EMA | Major trend separator — above = bull, below = bear |

---

## The Golden Cross & Death Cross

**Golden Cross:** 50 EMA crosses above 200 EMA → Bullish signal
**Death Cross:** 50 EMA crosses below 200 EMA → Bearish signal

These are powerful signals on daily and weekly charts.

---

## The Mentor's Rule on Moving Averages

**Only trade in the direction of the 200 EMA.**

If price is above 200 EMA → Only look for buy opportunities.
If price is below 200 EMA → Avoid buying. Wait or look for shorts.

# 4.27 — The Trade Setup — Entry, Target, Stop Loss

**Phase:** [[📒 Phase 4 MOC — Technical Analysis & Timing]]

---

## The Sacred Rule

**NEVER enter a trade without knowing your:**
1. Entry price
2. Target price
3. Stop loss price

If you don't have all three before entering, **you are gambling, not trading.**

---

## The Template

```
Stock: [Name]
Date: [Date]
Setup: [Pattern/Signal]
Entry: ₹[X]
Stop Loss: ₹[Y]   → Risk = ₹(X-Y) per share
Target: ₹[Z]      → Reward = ₹(Z-X) per share
R:R Ratio: [Reward ÷ Risk] → Must be at least 1.5
Quantity: [Shares]
Capital at Risk: ₹[Qty × (X-Y)] → Max 2% of total capital
```

---

## Example

```
Stock: HDFC Bank
Date: 15 Jan 2024
Setup: Bullish engulfing at 50 EMA support
Entry: ₹1,620
Stop Loss: ₹1,590   → Risk = ₹30/share
Target: ₹1,710      → Reward = ₹90/share
R:R = 3:1 ✅
Quantity: 10 shares
Capital at Risk: ₹300 (0.3% of ₹1 lakh portfolio) ✅
```

---

## The 2% Rule

**Never risk more than 2% of your total trading capital on a single trade.**

Capital: ₹1,00,000 → Max risk per trade = ₹2,000
If stop loss distance = ₹50 → Max qty = 2000 ÷ 50 = 40 shares

# 4.41 — The Trading Journal — Your Biggest Edge

**Phase:** [[📒 Phase 4 MOC — Technical Analysis & Timing]]

---

## Why a Journal is Not Optional

Most traders who fail → never journaled.
Most traders who succeed → journaled obsessively.

The journal turns **random trading into a system.**
It shows you your own patterns — your strengths and your recurring mistakes.

---

## What to Record in Every Trade

```
Date:
Stock:
Setup/Reason for Entry:
Entry Price:
Stop Loss:
Target:
R:R:
Exit Price:
P&L:
Emotion at Entry (1–10 confidence):
What went right:
What went wrong:
What I'll do differently:
Screenshot: [Chart image]
```

---

## What to Review Weekly

- Total trades: Win/Loss count
- Win Rate
- Average R:R on winning trades
- Average R:R on losing trades
- Most common mistake this week
- Best setup this week

---

## The Truth About Journaling

You will discover that 80% of your losses come from 20% of your bad habits.
The journal finds those habits.
Once found, you fix them.
That's how traders actually improve.
