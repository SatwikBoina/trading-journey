# 2.1 — What is a Stock and Why Companies Issue Them

**Phase:** [[📗 Phase 2 MOC — Market Literacy]]

---

## The Simple Explanation

Imagine you start a chai stall with ₹10,000.
It grows well. Now you want to open 10 more stalls. You need ₹10,00,000.
You don't have it. Bank loan is expensive.

**Solution:** You divide ownership of your business into 10,000 pieces.
Each piece = ₹100.
You sell 5,000 pieces to the public = you raise ₹5,00,000.
Those 5,000 people are now **shareholders** — they own part of your chai stall.

**That piece of ownership = a STOCK (also called a Share or Equity).**

---

## Why a Company Issues Stocks

1. Raise money without taking loans
2. Grow faster
3. Reward early employees and investors

When the business grows, the stock price goes up.
**Shareholders make money.**

When the business fails, the stock price falls.
**Shareholders lose money.**

---

## Key Truth

You are NOT buying a lottery ticket when you buy a stock.
You are buying **a piece of a real business.**

Think like a **business owner**, not a gambler.

# 2.3 — How Stock Prices Are Determined

**Phase:** [[📗 Phase 2 MOC — Market Literacy]]

---

## The Short Answer

**Supply and Demand.**

If more people want to buy a stock than sell it → price goes UP.
If more people want to sell than buy → price goes DOWN.

---

## What Drives Buying and Selling?

| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Company earns more profit | Price goes UP |
| Company reports losses | Price goes DOWN |
| RBI cuts interest rates | Market goes UP |
| Inflation rises unexpectedly | Market goes DOWN |
| FIIs buying heavily | Price goes UP |
| Government policy changes | Depends on sector |
| Global markets fall (USA crash) | India also falls |
| War/crisis news | Short-term fall |

---

## Important: Short-term vs Long-term

**Short-term:** Price is driven by news, emotions, rumors.
**Long-term:** Price follows the company's actual earnings and growth.

As Warren Buffett said:
*"In the short run, the market is a voting machine. In the long run, it is a weighing machine."*

# 2.4 — What is NIFTY 50 and SENSEX

**Phase:** [[📗 Phase 2 MOC — Market Literacy]]

---

## NIFTY 50

- Full name: NSE Nifty 50 Index
- Exchange: National Stock Exchange (NSE)
- Consists of: Top 50 companies by market cap in India
- Examples: Reliance, TCS, HDFC Bank, Infosys, ICICI Bank

**Think of NIFTY as the "temperature of the Indian economy."**
If NIFTY goes up → generally, India's biggest companies are doing well.
If NIFTY falls → market sentiment is negative or economy is slowing.

---

## SENSEX

- Full name: S&P BSE SENSEX
- Exchange: Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE)
- Consists of: Top 30 companies by market cap
- Older index — started in 1986

Both NIFTY and SENSEX move similarly because they contain the same large companies.

---

## Why This Matters For You

When you invest in a **NIFTY 50 Index Fund:**
→ You own a tiny piece of all 50 companies.
→ As India grows, NIFTY grows, your money grows.

This is the safest, simplest way for a beginner to grow wealth.

# 2.7 — Who Are FIIs and DIIs

**Phase:** [[📗 Phase 2 MOC — Market Literacy]]

---

## FII — Foreign Institutional Investor

These are **foreign funds** investing in Indian markets.
Examples: Vanguard, BlackRock, Foreign pension funds

They bring large amounts of foreign currency (dollars) into India.

**When FIIs buy** → Markets go UP (lot of money coming in)
**When FIIs sell** → Markets fall (money going out of India)

You can track FII/DII data daily on NSE website.

---

## DII — Domestic Institutional Investor

These are **Indian institutions** investing in markets.
Examples: LIC, Mutual Funds (SBI MF, HDFC MF), Pension Funds

When FIIs sell and market falls, **DIIs often buy** — acting as a cushion.
This is why Indian markets have become more stable in recent years.

---

## Why This Matters

FII activity = big market mover.
Check FII/DII data if market moves suddenly — it explains a lot.

Free data at: https://www.niftyindices.com or NSE website.

# 2.21 — Investor vs Trader — Two Very Different Mindsets

**Phase:** [[📗 Phase 2 MOC — Market Literacy]]

---

## The Core Difference

| | Investor | Trader |
|--|----------|--------|
| Timeframe | Years to decades | Minutes to months |
| Focus | Business quality | Price movements |
| Tools | Fundamentals (PE, EPS, Revenue) | Charts (Technical Analysis) |
| Risk | Lower (time dilutes risk) | Higher |
| Skill needed | Patience + Research | Speed + Discipline |
| Who should do it | Beginners + Everyone | Only after mastering Phase 3 & 4 |

Trading looks exciting. Investing looks boring.
Trading makes good YouTube content. Investing makes actual wealth.

Most people who got rich in the stock market got rich by:
- Buying good businesses
- Holding them for 10–20 years
- Doing nothing while the market did the work

---

## The Order

```
Investor (Phase 3) → Swing Trader (Phase 4) → Advanced Trader (Phase 5)
```

Do NOT reverse this order.

# 2.26 — Why 90% of Retail Traders Lose Money

**Phase:** [[📗 Phase 2 MOC — Market Literacy]]

---

## The Brutal Truth

SEBI's own study showed:
- 9 out of 10 F&O traders lose money
- Average loss per retail trader in F&O: ₹1.1 lakh/year
- Professional traders take money from retail traders

---

## Why They Lose

1. **No edge** — they act on tips, news, gut feeling
2. **No risk management** — one bad trade wipes out 10 good trades
3. **Overtrading** — more trades = more losses + more brokerage
4. **Emotions** — fear and greed drive every decision
5. **No system** — they improvise every day
6. **Undercapitalized** — can't survive the natural losing streaks

---

## What This Means For You

You are NOT starting as a trader.
You are starting as an investor.

Investing is where the actual wealth is built.
Trading is only for people who have:
- Mastered fundamentals
- Mastered technical analysis
- Built a trading system
- Have risk management in place

That is Phase 4 and 5. Not today.
