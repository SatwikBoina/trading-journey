# E2.7 — Repo Rate — RBI's Most Powerful Tool

**Module:** [[🔵 Econ Module 2 MOC — Money Banking & Interest Rates]]

---

## What is the Repo Rate?

**Repo = Repurchase Agreement**

When banks need money urgently, they borrow from RBI.
The rate RBI charges banks for this borrowing = **Repo Rate.**

As of 2024, India's Repo Rate = **6.5%**

---

## The Simple Analogy

Think of RBI as the "bank for banks."
Just like you borrow from SBI at 8–9% interest,
SBI borrows from RBI at 6.5% (repo rate).

When RBI changes this rate, it changes the cost of money for ALL banks.
Which changes the cost of money for ALL of India.

---

## How Repo Rate Ripples Through the Economy

```
RBI Repo Rate: 6.5%
  ↓
SBI Home Loan: ~8.5% (Repo + bank's spread)
  ↓
Your EMI on ₹50 lakh home loan: ₹43,000/month

If RBI raises Repo Rate to 7%:
  ↓
SBI Home Loan becomes: ~9%
  ↓
Your EMI rises to ₹45,000/month

You borrow less. Developer sells fewer homes.
Real estate and banking stocks fall.
```

---

## The Three Scenarios

| RBI Action | Economy Signal | Market Impact |
|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| Rate Hike | Inflation too high — cooling economy | Markets fall short-term |
| Rate Cut | Economy slow — stimulating growth | Markets rally |
| Rate Hold | Economy balanced | Neutral / mild positive |

---

## RBI's Monetary Policy Stance

Along with the rate, RBI signals its **stance:**

- **Accommodative** = More rate cuts coming. Very bullish for markets.
- **Neutral** = Could go either way. Wait and watch.
- **Hawkish** = More rate hikes possible. Bearish for rate-sensitive stocks.

**Watch the stance. It tells you the direction more than the rate itself.**

# E2.12 — Why Rising Rates Hurt Stock Valuations

**Module:** [[🔵 Econ Module 2 MOC — Money Banking & Interest Rates]]

---

## The Discount Rate Concept

This is the mathematical reason why interest rates and stock prices are inversely related.

A stock's price = Present value of all future earnings.
To calculate "present value," you use a **discount rate** — which is based on interest rates.

**Higher interest rate → Higher discount rate → Future earnings worth LESS today → Stock price falls**

---

## Simple Example

A company will earn ₹100 next year.

If discount rate is 6%:
Present Value = 100 ÷ 1.06 = **₹94.3** (stock is worth this today)

If discount rate rises to 10%:
Present Value = 100 ÷ 1.10 = **₹90.9** (stock is worth LESS)

Same company. Same future earnings. But the stock is cheaper just because rates rose.

---

## Why This Hits Growth Stocks Harder

Growth stocks (tech, new-age companies) earn very little TODAY but are expected to earn a LOT in the future.

When rates rise, all those future earnings get discounted more heavily.
That's why growth/tech stocks fall the MOST during rate hike cycles.

Value stocks (FMCG, utilities) earn steadily NOW.
Their discounting impact is smaller.

**Rate hike cycle → Rotate from growth to value.**

---

## The Alternative Asset Argument

When risk-free FD rate = 4% → People prefer stocks (higher potential return)
When risk-free FD rate = 8% → Why take stock market risk? Park it in FD.

Rising rates make FDs and bonds more attractive vs stocks.
Money flows out of stocks into bonds/FDs.
NIFTY falls.

# E2.16 — Bond Yields and Stock Markets — The Seesaw

**Module:** [[🔵 Econ Module 2 MOC — Money Banking & Interest Rates]]

---

## What is a Bond Yield?

**Bond** = Loan to the government. Government pays you interest.

**Bond Yield** = The effective interest rate you earn on a bond.

India's 10-year Government Bond (G-Sec) yield = Currently ~7%.
This means: Lend ₹100 to India's government for 10 years, get ₹7/year in interest.

---

## The Price-Yield Inverse Relationship

Bond price and bond yield move in **opposite directions.**

If bond price rises → yield falls.
If bond price falls → yield rises.

This is mathematical. Fixed coupon ÷ rising price = lower yield.

---

## Bond Yield vs Stock Market — The Seesaw

These two asset classes compete for the same money.

**When bond yields rise:**
- Bonds become more attractive (safe + decent return)
- Investors sell stocks to buy bonds
- Stock market falls
- This is called "yield rising hurts equities"

**When bond yields fall:**
- Bonds become less attractive
- Investors shift to stocks for better returns
- Stock market rises

---

## The 10-Year G-Sec — India's Most Watched Yield

This is the benchmark. Track it at:
- RBI website → Government Securities
- Investing.com → India 10-Year Bond

When 10-year yield shoots up (say from 6.8% to 7.5%):
→ Expect banking and rate-sensitive sectors to feel pressure.
→ NIFTY may see selling.
