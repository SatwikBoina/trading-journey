# E1.2 — GDP — The Scoreboard of a Country

**Module:** [[🟢 Econ Module 1 MOC — How Economies Work]]

---

## What is GDP?

**GDP = Gross Domestic Product**

It is the **total rupee value of all goods and services produced inside India in one year.**

Think of it as India's annual report card.
Just like a company reports revenue every quarter, a country reports GDP every quarter.

---

## Simple Example

Imagine India only produces 3 things:
- Chai: ₹100 crore worth
- Software: ₹500 crore worth
- Steel: ₹200 crore worth

**India's GDP = ₹800 crore**

In reality, India's GDP = ~₹300 lakh crore (as of 2024). India is the 5th largest economy in the world.

---

## The Four Components of GDP

**GDP = C + I + G + NX**

| Letter | Stands For | Meaning |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| C | Consumption | What people buy — food, clothes, electronics |
| I | Investment | What companies spend on factories, machines, buildings |
| G | Government Spending | What govt spends on roads, defence, schemes |
| NX | Net Exports | Exports minus Imports |

In India, **C (consumption)** is the biggest driver. India is a consumption-led economy. When Indians spend more → GDP grows → markets go up.

---

## GDP Growth Rate — What Actually Matters

Investors don't look at GDP in rupees. They look at **GDP growth rate.**

- India's "good" growth: **6–8% per year**
- Below 5%: Concern. Market may fall.
- Above 8%: Strong. Market usually rallies.
- Negative GDP for 2 consecutive quarters: **Recession**

India's GDP growth target is to hit **$5 trillion economy** by 2027–28.

---

## Why GDP Matters for Your Stocks

Higher GDP growth → companies earn more → earnings per share grows → stock prices rise.
Lower GDP growth → companies struggle → earnings fall → stock prices fall.

**GDP is the tide. All stocks are boats.**

# E1.6 — Business Cycles — Expansion, Peak, Recession, Recovery

**Module:** [[🟢 Econ Module 1 MOC — How Economies Work]]

---

## The Four Phases

Every economy goes through this cycle repeatedly. It is natural. It is predictable. And it is tradeable.

```
EXPANSION → PEAK → CONTRACTION (Recession) → TROUGH → EXPANSION again
```

### 1. Expansion Phase
- GDP growing fast
- Companies hiring
- People spending more
- Stock market rising
- Credit easy to get
- Confidence high

**Best sectors:** IT, Auto, Real Estate, Consumer Discretionary, Banks

### 2. Peak Phase
- Growth at maximum
- Inflation rising (too much demand)
- RBI starts raising rates
- Market near all-time highs
- Everyone feels rich and optimistic

**Danger zone:** Overvaluation. Market ignores bad news.

### 3. Contraction / Recession
- GDP slowing or negative
- Companies laying off employees
- Consumers cutting spending
- Stock market falling
- Banks tightening credit
- Confidence collapsing

**Best sectors:** FMCG, Pharma, Utilities (people still buy soap, medicine, pay electricity bills)

### 4. Trough / Recovery
- Economy at its lowest
- Fear at maximum
- RBI cuts rates to stimulate
- Smart investors start buying
- Market bottoms out (often before economy recovers)

**This is the best time to invest — but it feels the most terrifying.**

---

## The Critical Insight for Investors

> **The stock market leads the economy by 6–9 months.**

The market starts falling BEFORE the recession is officially declared.
The market starts rising BEFORE the economy officially recovers.

**By the time news says "recession is here" — the market has already fallen 30–40%.**
**By the time news says "economy is recovering" — the market has already risen 30–40%.**

This is why investing on news is always late.

---

## India's Recent Cycles

| Period | Phase | NIFTY Behaviour |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| 2003–2007 | Strong Expansion | NIFTY 5x |
| 2008 | Deep Recession (global) | NIFTY fell 60% |
| 2009–2010 | Recovery | NIFTY doubled |
| 2020 (COVID) | Sudden Contraction | NIFTY fell 38% in 6 weeks |
| 2020–2021 | Fastest Recovery | NIFTY doubled in 18 months |

# E1.11 — Supply and Demand — The Core of All Economics

**Module:** [[🟢 Econ Module 1 MOC — How Economies Work]]

---

## The Single Most Important Concept in Economics

Every price in the world — whether it's onions, crude oil, a stock, or a house — is determined by **supply and demand.**

**Demand** = How much buyers want something
**Supply** = How much sellers have to offer

---

## The Laws

**Law of Demand:** When price goes UP → demand goes DOWN (people buy less)
**Law of Supply:** When price goes UP → supply goes UP (sellers want to sell more)

**Equilibrium:** The price where supply equals demand. The market's natural resting point.

---

## Real Market Examples

| Situation | Supply/Demand Change | Price Effect |
|-----------|---------------------|-------------|
| India has a bad monsoon | Supply of food falls | Food prices rise → inflation |
| Crude oil supply cut by OPEC | Oil supply falls | Crude rises → fuel, transport costs rise |
| RBI prints more money | Money supply rises | Each rupee is worth less → inflation |
| Company posts bad results | Demand for stock falls | Stock price falls |
| FIIs start buying Indian stocks heavily | Demand for Indian stocks rises | Market goes up |

---

## Why This Matters Directly for Investing

Every stock price is a supply-demand equation:
- More buyers than sellers → price goes up
- More sellers than buyers → price goes down

But underlying the stock's supply-demand is the company's product supply-demand:
- If the company's product demand is rising → revenue rises → stock goes up (eventually)
- If a company's product is being replaced → demand falling → stock falls (eventually)

**Invest in companies where product demand is structurally rising.**

# E1.16 — PMI — The Real-Time Economy Signal

**Module:** [[🟢 Econ Module 1 MOC — How Economies Work]]

---

## What is PMI?

**Purchasing Managers Index** — A monthly survey of business managers asking:
*"Is your business activity better, same, or worse than last month?"*

Released every month. **One of the most important real-time economic signals.**

---

## How to Read It

| PMI Value | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| Above 50 | Economy expanding (positive for markets) |
| Exactly 50 | No change — flat |
| Below 50 | Economy contracting (negative for markets) |

The **higher above 50**, the faster the expansion.
The **lower below 50**, the deeper the contraction.

---

## Two Types of PMI

**Manufacturing PMI** — Factory orders, production, employment
**Services PMI** — Service sector activity (India's biggest sector)

India's PMI is typically strong. India Manufacturing PMI has been above 55 for most of 2023–2024 — indicating strong factory growth.

---

## Where to Track

- Investing.com → Economic Calendar → India PMI
- Released on 1st business day of every month (Manufacturing)
- Released a few days later (Services)

---

## Market Reaction to PMI

PMI comes in at 58 (higher than expected 55) → Market rallies (economy stronger than thought)
PMI comes in at 48 (lower than expected 52) → Market falls (contraction signal)
